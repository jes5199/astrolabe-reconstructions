# Sources and credits

Each drawing was prepared with reference to the sources below. The photographs were consulted for study only. None is reproduced here, in whole or in part, and no drawing is a tracing.

## Isfahan, 984/5 or 1003/4

Prepared with reference to: History of Science Museum, University of Oxford, inv. 33767, astrolabe by Ahmad and Muhammad, sons of Ibrahim, Isfahan, 984/5 or 1003/4 (A.H. 374 or 394); images in the Oxford Astrolabe Catalogue. Wording of inscriptions and star identifications from the same catalogue.

## Byzantine, 1062

Prepared with reference to: the astrolabe of Sergios, 1062, Brescia; O. M. Dalton, "The Byzantine Astrolabe at Brescia", *Proceedings of the British Academy* 12 (1926), plates I and II. Star names, zodiac names and verses as Dalton prints them. The stem and ring were drawn with reference to the photograph of the instrument in the online catalogue of the Galileo exhibition at Palazzo Strozzi (Istituto e Museo di Storia della Scienza, Florence, © 2009; no photographer is named), where it is listed as "Anonymous Astrolabe, 1062, Brescia, Civici Musei d'Arte e Storia, inv. IC n. 2". The rete's proportions were drawn with reference to the photograph of the rete printed as Fig. C1 ("The rete of the 1062 astrolabe", p. 221) in "Notes on the 1062 astrolabe in Brescia", in David A. King, "The Byzantine Astrolabe of 1062" (2007/2018); that photograph is credited there as courtesy of the Museo dell'Età Cristiana, Brescia (now the Santa Giulia Museo della Città). The two sources give different inventory numbers for the instrument: IC n. 2 in the exhibition catalogue, 36 in the notes just cited.

## Toledo, 1068

Prepared with reference to: astrolabe by Ibrahim ibn Sa'id al-Sahli, Toledo, August 1068 (Shawwal 460); History of Science Museum, University of Oxford, inv. 55331; images in the Oxford Astrolabe Catalogue. Wording of the zodiac names and plate inscriptions from the same catalogue.

## England, 1326

Prepared with reference to: British Museum, 1909,0617.1; a photograph by Marie-Lan Nguyen (Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 2.5) and a British Museum image.

## England (?), c. 1370, front and back

Prepared with reference to: History of Science Museum, University of Oxford, inv. 47869; images in the Oxford Astrolabe Catalogue. Spellings of the names as the catalogue transcribes them.

## Lettering

The published SVG files carry their lettering as outlines, so no font is needed to view them. Before outlining, some lettering was set in these open typefaces:

| Typeface | Used for |
|---|---|
| Noto Kufi Arabic | Isfahan and Toledo: zodiac and star names, limb numerals, plate lettering |
| Gentium Plus | Byzantine: the rete's zodiac names, star names and verses |
| EB Garamond | England 1326: the degree numbers of the zodiac band |
| Manufacturing Consent | England c. 1370, front and back: the small Gothic letters |
| Inter | the caption at the foot of every sheet |

Much of the lettering is not type but built from strokes of our own, read from the photographs:

- Isfahan: the crossed lam-alif in two names.
- Byzantine: every letter on the limb and the plate, a pen alphabet of 22 plain Greek capitals.
- Toledo: the stretched letters of the zodiac names, the crossed lam-alif, and the waw and mim of the Gemini name.
- England 1326: the twelve zodiac names and five star names of the rete, the plate's inscription, and all ten figures of the limb and the plate.
- England c. 1370, front: the capitals of the sign and star names, the sign for the ending -us, the 23 hour letters of the limb with their cross and lozenges, and the plate's figures.
- England c. 1370, back: the capitals, the figures, the -us sign, the abbreviation bar, the leaf spray and the points.
