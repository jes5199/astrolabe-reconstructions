# astrolabe-reconstructions

Constructed drawings of five historical astrolabes, with a note for each saying what was read, what was continued by rule, what was inferred and what was left out.

An astrolabe is a flat model of the sky. A pierced star map, the rete, turns over a plate (or tympan) engraved for one latitude; both sit in the body of the instrument, whose raised rim, the limb, carries the scales. The drawings were made for a short film about Chaucer's *Treatise on the Astrolabe*.

## How they were made

Photographs were used as reference only. Each feature was measured on a photograph and then constructed: circles and scales from geometry, the plates and star maps by stereographic projection, figures and letters from pen strokes of our own. Nothing is traced, and no photograph or part of one is included.

These are idealized reconstructions, not facsimiles. Circles are exact, wear is mostly left out, and parts that are damaged or hidden are continued by the rule the visible parts follow. [METHOD.md](METHOD.md) gives the procedure and its limits.

## How far to trust them

Trust the layout: which scales an instrument has, how they are numbered, what is written where, and the shapes of the parts. Each note separates what was read from what was continued by rule or inferred, and names the conjectural parts.

Do not measure the original from these drawings. Uneven spacing has been made even, and the measurements come from one or two photographs, never from the object. Where the working measurement had an uncertainty, the note gives it.
