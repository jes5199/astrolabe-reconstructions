# England, dated 1326 (British Museum, 1909,0617.1)

Drawing: [svg](../drawings/england-1326-british-museum-1909-0617-1.svg), [png](../drawings/england-1326-british-museum-1909-0617-1.png)

## The instrument

A brass astrolabe, England, dated 1326, in the British Museum. It has a plate for Oxford, and many of its star pointers are shaped as animals and heads: a dog's head for Sirius, a dragon's head for Antares, a small bird for Vega.

## What the drawing shows

The front: the rete (the pierced star map) over the plate (tympan) for Oxford, latitude 51°50′, inside the limb (the rim with the degree scale), with the throne, shackle and ring above.

## Read from the photographs

- The rete from two photographs: one of the assembled instrument, which fixes every pointer's tip, and one of the rete taken apart, which shows the shapes that the rule hides in the first. There are 34 pointer tips. Comparing them with the stars' places in 1326 identifies 32, some doubtfully; two are unidentified and kept.
- The figures: the dog, the dragon's head and tail, two birds, two human heads and several duck heads.
- The zodiac band: all twelve names in the engraver's hand, each where he put it and with its top towards the centre of the ring, so that the four at the top stand upside down to the viewer, as on the instrument. A cell every 6° numbered 6 to 30 in each sign, ticks every 2°.
- Five star names on the outer band, in his smaller hand: Alhabor (by the dog), Markeb, algorab, batnachaythos and Cenok. Alhabor and Pisces were read letter by letter; the other fifteen names are built from the same letter forms inside the lengths and heights measured for each.
- The limb: a figure in every five-degree cell, 5 to 360 clockwise, tops outwards, in the engraver's own forms. Seventy were read; 85 and 285 are partly hidden by the clips of the display stand. A dot on every 15° line; one-degree ticks.
- The plate: "51 50" over "Latitudo Oxonie", read on the photograph; almucantars (circles of equal altitude) every 3°; azimuth circles every 5°; unequal hours 1 to 12.
- The throne: a low shoulder on the limb, an egg-shaped ball, a shackle and the ring.

## Continued by rule

- Azimuth circles above about 75° of altitude, where the photograph no longer resolves them.
- Up the meridian, the altitude figures 39 to 48 (18 to 36 were read). Some azimuth figures; the hour figure 1.
- The numerals on the left side of the plate, which show less clearly, are drawn as the mirror of the right.

## Inferred

- The doubtful star identifications.
- Which way the plate's side numerals stand could not be read. They are drawn like the meridian's.
- The ring is drawn standing up; on the photograph of the assembled instrument it hangs behind the ball.

## Left out

The rule, which would hide the rete; the pin and wedge; the back. Altitude figures above 48° on the plate. Lettering on the rete's arms, hub and tendrils, which is there but could not be read. The engraver's variation from cell to cell: his figures differ, ours are ten fixed forms.
