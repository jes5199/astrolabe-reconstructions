# Isfahan, 984/5 or 1003/4 (Oxford, inv. 33767)

Drawing: [svg](../drawings/isfahan-984-oxford-33767.svg), [png](../drawings/isfahan-984-oxford-33767.png)

## The instrument

An astrolabe by Ahmad and Muhammad, sons of Ibrahim, of Isfahan, now in the History of Science Museum, University of Oxford. The museum's catalogue dates it 984/5 or 1003/4 (A.H. 374 or 394): two readings of the year in the signature. The rete, the pierced star map on top, is badly corroded.

## What the drawing shows

The front: the rete over the maker's plate (tympan) for latitude 32°, his nearest to Isfahan, inside the limb (the rim with the degree scale), with the throne and ring above. The rete has 41 pointers: the 38 stars of the museum's report and three more that are unidentified.

## Read from the photographs

- The rete's whole layout, the form of each pointer and the place of its tip.
- The zodiac names in the cells that survive, stretched as engraved, tops to the band's outer edge all the way round.
- The throne: a triangle pierced with eight rows of arches (2, 3, 4, then 3, 4, 5, 6, 7); the ring's top stands 1.21 limb radii above the limb.
- On the limb, 26 of the 72 numerals, in the abjad (letter-numeral) system: one in each five-degree cell, 5 to 360 clockwise from the top, tops outwards, written without dots.
- On the plate: latitude 32 as engraved ("latitude 32, its hours 14 8"); almucantars (circles of equal altitude) every 6°; eleven unequal-hour lines; azimuth circles below the horizon only; a dotted line for dawn and twilight.

## Continued by rule

- The other 46 limb numerals, hidden by corrosion, follow the run of the 26 that were read.
- The plate's numerals follow the rule the legible ones show.
- Lost pointers (Antares and Rasalhague among them) are redrawn with their tips at the stars' computed places. The places are computed for 985; the two possible dates differ by about 0.26° of precession.

## Inferred

- Four zodiac names stand in cells that are corroded away, and several star names are illegible. These are restored from the standard names and from the catalogue's identifications.
- One limb cell (180 to 185) shows a stroke on the photographs that the rule's numeral does not explain. It is drawn by the rule.

## Left out

Corrosion and wear. The pin and its horse, which would hide the hub. The rete's handle. The back. Any degree scale on the zodiac band, which is unreadable. Two slanting lines beside the words "East" and "West" on the plate, which we could not identify; the words are placed, the lines are not.
