# England (?), c. 1370: the back (Oxford, inv. 47869)

Drawing: [svg](../drawings/england-c1370-oxford-47869-back.svg), [png](../drawings/england-c1370-oxford-47869-back.png)

## The instrument

The back of the astrolabe whose front is described in [its own note](england-c1370-oxford-47869.md). The museum's catalogue lists five scales on it: degrees, zodiacal signs, calendar, saints' days and a shadow square. It describes the alidade (the sighting rule) as original.

## What the drawing shows

From the rim inwards: an altitude scale in four quadrants; a ladder of 360 degrees; the zodiac with its sign names; the calendar with 365 day cells and the month names; three saints for each month with their days and Sunday letters; and, in the lower half, the shadow square. The alidade is drawn at altitude 0.

## Read from the photographs

- Every scale, its radius and its numbering; the counts (360 degrees, 365 days, the month lengths); all names and abbreviations.
- Thirty-six saints with their days and Sunday (dominical) letters. All 36 letters agree with the letters computed from the 36 days, so the two readings check each other.
- Two irregularities in the altitude numbering, both kept: in the lower left the last numeral is 80 where the rule gives 85, and the first cell above the west point carries a second 5.
- The first point of Aries falls at 11.55 days of March elapsed, the middle of the cell of 12 March. The uncertainty is about 0.3 day.
- The day cells are unequal: a day is 0.955° wide at the top of the ring (June) and 1.019° at the bottom (December). The ring itself is concentric, so the maker divided it unequally to follow the sun.

## Continued by rule

- Degrees and five-degree cells are exactly equal.
- The 365 days are set out equally on an eccentric circle and read from the centre. The circle is fitted to the twelve dates on which the signs begin (eccentricity 0.0392, residual 0.12 day), and the drawing is within 0.21 day of each. This figure is not settled: a fit to all the day lines gives 0.0315. A square-on photograph would decide it.
- The saints are spaced evenly across each month.

## Inferred

- The alidade's sighting vanes in plan. The photographs show them standing, in perspective.
- Which way the shadow square's figures stand on its sides.
- Some readings are doubtful: the ending of Gemini's abbreviation, and the saints "anu" (March), "pe.p" (June) and "mari" and "mathe" (September).

## Left out

Wear and scratches. The maker's uneven spacing, apart from the unequal days. His twenty-four leaf sprays, each different; one spray of ours stands in every cell. The wedge through the pin and a notch in the rim at the top. Nothing was checked against the object: there is one photograph of the back alone and one of the assembly.
