# Method

## In short

Photographs were used as reference only. Every line in these drawings was measured and then constructed, from geometry (stereographic projection, circles, scales) and from pen strokes of our own. Nothing is traced. No photograph, and no part of one, is included in any file.

The drawings are idealized reconstructions, not facsimiles. Wear and the makers' irregularities are mostly left out. Damaged or hidden parts are continued by rule. Conjectural parts are named in each note.

## Terms

- **Rete**: the pierced plate on top, a map of the stars and of the sun's yearly path (the zodiac band). Its pointers mark stars.
- **Plate**, or **tympan**: the disc under the rete, engraved for one latitude.
- **Almucantar**: a circle of equal altitude on the plate. The horizon is the lowest.
- **Limb**: the raised rim of the instrument's body, carrying the degree scale.
- **Throne**: the shaped piece at the top that joins the body to its shackle and ring.
- **Alidade**: the sighting rule on the back.

## Measuring

Each photograph was first registered: the centre found, a known circle fitted, the turn of the instrument in the frame measured. Features were then read in two ways. Brightness was sampled along rays, circles and lines to find edges, count divisions and measure widths. Small details were read by eye on enlarged, gridded crops, often turned level or unrolled so that a ring became a straight strip.

The readings are numbers: a radius as a fraction of the limb, the angle of a tip, the count of cells in a scale, the height of a letter. No photograph was converted into an outline.

## Constructing

The numbers were then drawn afresh. Plates are computed by stereographic projection for the latitude engraved or measured on the plate. Scales are divided exactly. Bars and bands have even widths and true arcs. Pointers, animals and ornaments are built from a few measured points joined by our own curves.

Star pointers follow one of two rules, stated in each note. On most drawings every tip stands where the maker put it, as read on the photograph. On the Byzantine drawing each tip stands on its star's computed place for 1062.

As a check, the constructed lines were laid over the photograph. On several plates this decided what a family of lines was: computed unequal-hour lines or azimuth circles either fall on the engraved lines or they do not.

## Lettering

Some lettering is set in an open typeface chosen to be near the engraver's hand, with the wording from the museum's catalogue or the publication. Where no typeface had the engraver's forms, the letters or figures were built from strokes, each read on its own cell. [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md) lists which is which.

## What "idealized" costs

A reader should not measure the original from these drawings.

- Circles are exact and divisions equal. The makers' are not: on one limb the hour marks stand up to 1.6 degrees from their even places, and on one calendar the day cells vary by design.
- Wear, corrosion, scratches and repairs are not drawn. A corroded numeral is replaced by the one its scale requires.
- Some shapes are simplified so that they read at small size, and a few letters are opened or shortened for legibility. The notes say where.
- Proportions come from one or two photographs of each part, not from the object. A slightly oblique photograph carries its error into the reading.
- Colours are ours: flat brass and dark engraving, with the plate's lines kept quiet so that the rete reads first.

What the drawings do preserve is the logic of each instrument: its scales and their numbering, the layout of the rete, the latitude of the plate, the wording of the inscriptions as far as they could be read.

## The four categories

Each note sorts its drawing into four kinds of statement.

- **Read from the photographs**: seen and measured.
- **Continued by rule**: not seen everywhere, but fixed by a rule the visible parts show, such as the next numeral in a run.
- **Inferred**: a judgement from indirect evidence. These are the conjectural parts.
- **Left out**: seen or known but not drawn.
